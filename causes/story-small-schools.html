Story from the field — School Support

Where learning begins: two Montessori classes bring early education to Angkor's children

October 2026

Near the Angkor temples, 58 children aged three to five now start their school life in a bright, calm classroom where they choose their own activities, work with their hands and learn in two languages — free of charge.

A pilot built on 29 years of experience

For nearly three decades Bayon School has offered children from poor families in the Angkor region free schooling, from preschool all the way to university. Its newest step is a pilot of two bilingual Khmer–English Montessori preschool classes that follow the Cambodian national curriculum.

Why the early years matter

In Cambodia, a good and affordable preschool is still out of reach for many families. Yet the early years shape a child's curiosity, confidence and independence. In these classes, children learn by choosing, experimenting and handling real materials, which builds creativity, critical thinking and self-esteem.

A head start in English and in confidence

Children are introduced to English from an early age alongside Khmer, a skill that can open doors later in school and work. Just as important, they learn in a safe and caring place, which is often the first steady classroom many of them have known.

“A small school is a promise that a child in a faraway village has not been forgotten. Keeping its doors open is how we keep that promise.”
— Christophe, founder of Entraide Asia

Source information: Bayon School (ecoledubayon.org)
