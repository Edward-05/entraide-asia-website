Keeping Students in School Long-Term

One term of support rarely changes a family's situation. This cause is about staying with a student for as many years as it takes.

Why It Matters

Dropout risk doesn't end after one good year

A family's circumstances change year to year — a bad harvest, an illness, a sibling who needs support too. A student who was safely enrolled last year can be at risk again the next. Short-term aid can get a student through a single crisis, but it doesn't remove the underlying risk that pulls students out of school one grade at a time.

Entraide Asia treats long-term enrollment as something to actively manage, not assume — following up year after year with the same students and families rather than funding a single school year and moving on.

How This Works

What multi-year support looks like

1

Household Allowances

Modest, regular payments to families so a child's education doesn't compete directly with the household budget each month.

2

Transport, Every Year

Bicycles and transport support renewed as students move up through secondary school, when the distance to class often grows.

3

Following Up in Person

Christophe and local partners check in with sponsored students directly, catching problems — illness, family hardship, falling grades — before they become a reason to drop out.

4

Through to University

For students who qualify, support extends past secondary school into university or vocational study, rather than stopping at graduation.

Long-term support means returning to check in — not a single donation and moving on.

Seen in Action

Bayon School and individual students in Myanmar

At Bayon School, secondary students are given a bicycle and a household allowance renewed each year, not just once. Christophe applies the same approach to individual students he sponsors directly in Myanmar — support that continues for as long as a student stays in school, checked on through in-person visits rather than a one-time grant.

How Entraide Asia Helps

Commitments measured in years, not terms

Where possible, support is structured as an ongoing commitment to a specific student or school rather than a one-off gift, because the risk of dropping out rarely disappears after a single term of help. Staying involved year after year is what actually keeps a student enrolled through to graduation.
